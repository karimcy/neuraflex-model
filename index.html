09 · Verification

Watch-outs before submission

Facts the research pass could not verify, or where the application's current wording needs correcting.

⚠ rewordThe 45GW UPS figure is additions, not stock. IEA Global Energy Review 2026: battery-based UPS capacity added in 2025 was 45GW (+30% YoY), primarily data centres. Q9 [3] currently reads as installed base, cite IEA and say "added".
⚠ verifyAtlasEdge is exiting UK colocation. It announced the sale of both UK data centres (London, Leeds) to Templus on 16 Dec 2025, completing H1 2026. Naming it in the UK cohort needs checking with them.
confirm"Colvore" has no public UK presence. The only similar entity found is Colovore, a US-only liquid-cooling colo. Confirm the entity name and its letter of support.
confirmElements Green is a solar/BESS developer. No public data-centre pipeline exists (14GW+ renewables portfolio, Staythorpe 360MW BESS). Phrase its role as renewables/BESS partner, or attribute DC capacity to a private engagement.
noteCapacity Market prices broke in March 2026. T-4 2029/30 cleared at £27.10/kW/yr vs £60–65 in the prior two auctions. The model defaults to the new regime; do not anchor revenue claims on the old prices.
noteModel behind-the-meter BESS on the storage derating table. The generic 85.58% DSR derating is under DESNZ reform consultation (splitting BTM storage into its own class). The model derates by duration (10.99%/h).
notePost-TCR, TNUoS residuals are fixed banded charges. Peak-avoidance value now sits only in DUoS red-band and wholesale spreads, legacy "Triad avoidance" £/MW figures are obsolete and the model's peak-avoidance adder is deliberately small.
⚠ reword"Data centres reach ~12% of GB peak by 2030 (NESO)" is unsupported. Two independent research passes could not source it, the circulating 12% figure is a US projection. Verified NESO figures to use instead: data-centre demand 5→22TWh by 2030 (CP2030 Annex 1, ≈7.7% of annual demand), FES 2025 DC peak 5.4GW by 2035, or the 73GW connection queue against a 45GW GB peak (Ofgem Curate).
⚠ re-attributeThe "2GW Consumer-Led Flexibility target" needs re-attribution, not deletion. Verified (Jul 2026 Roadmap update): "UKRI has committed £74m to the CLF Challenge, aiming to deliver at least 2GW of flexibility by 2030", this competition's own aim, the natural frame for NeuraFlex's contribution. The government policy range is separately 10–12GW CLF by 2030 (incl. 1.7GW non-domestic), and NESO targets 750MW additional non-domestic flexibility by 2030. Don't call 2GW a government target.
noteCooling flexibility is seasonal at water-free AI builds. The marquee 2024-26 designs (Microsoft's zero-water standard, Stargate, xAI Memphis) reject heat through dry coolers or air-cooled chillers and free-cool through UK winters, compressors off, cooling ~3-6% of IT - so winter constraint events at those sites must be firmed by the battery, with cooling flex (adiabatic assist, thermal storage, fans) a summer lever. Legacy air-cooled and chilled-water estates, including the pilot host, keep material winter chiller flex. Set the cooling-share input ~4-8% for water-free builds, 18-30% for legacy sites.
gapNo GB source publishes expected curtailment for demand-side non-firm connections. The regime is per-site DCP404 hours-caps with compensation above the cap, dispatched in real time (zero notice) with day-ahead forecast visibility. The model's scenario band (85–400 restricted hours/yr) comes from Duke's US tolerance study; real GB event distributions are registration-gated on the UKPN/SPEN open-data portals, worth pulling during the project. This gap is itself the market opportunity Q9 describes.
